# The method behind this map — and where it comes from

*For teams adopting JourneyMap. Every design choice below is either (a) drawn from established practice, or (b) an explicit, labelled adaptation. Nothing is presented as doctrine that isn't.*

## The three pillars the tool stands on

**1. Journey mapping & service design (the anatomy).**
The map's structure — stages left to right, with parallel layers describing *what the donor experiences*, *which touchpoints deliver it*, *what the organization does behind the scenes*, and *where things break or could improve* — is the standard anatomy of customer/donor journey mapping and service blueprinting:

- James Kalbach, *Mapping Experiences* (O'Reilly, 2016) — the foundational textbook: journey maps record experience across touchpoints per stage; "moments of truth" (Jan Carlzon) and opportunity bands are core elements.
- NN/g (Nielsen Norman Group), *Service Blueprints / Customer Journey Maps* — the frontstage / backstage distinction (what the customer sees vs. what makes it happen) is the direct source of our **Org actions** lane.
- G. Lynn Shostack, *Designing Services That Deliver* (HBR, 1984) — origin of service blueprinting, whose layers ours compress into four.

**2. Donor-centered fundraising (the donor-side content).**
Penelope Burk, *Donor-Centered Fundraising* (Cygnus, 2003; updated 2018) — the research-based classic: donors stay when they are thanked **promptly, specifically, informatively** ("the acknowledgement is the recognition"); they expect needs met *before* the next ask. This drives the questions under our lanes — *what does the donor think, feel, or decide at this stage?* — and the emphasis in our templates on first-gift thank-you speed and stewardship.

**3. Relationship fundraising & moves management (the practice discipline).**
- Ken Burnett, *Relationship Fundraising: A Donor-Based Approach to the Business of Raising Money* (White Lion Press, 1992; 2nd ed. Wiley, 2002) — donors as long-term relationships to be cultivated, not transactions.
- **Moves management** — developed by David Dunlop at Cornell University ("changing people's attitudes so they want to give"): identify → qualify → cultivate → solicit → steward. It underpins our **Leadership giving** template's stage axis and the no-ask cultivation cadence written into its cards. Moves management is institutionalized in the major-gifts modules of Raiser's Edge and comparable CRMs.

| **Donor experience** lane | Journey-map experience/emotion band (Kalbach); donor-side questions (Burk) | Canon |
| **Channels & touchpoints** lane | CJM touchpoints element (Kalbach; Carlzon moments of truth) | Canon |
| **Org actions** lane | Service blueprint backstage (Shostack; NN/g) | Canon |
| **Gaps & opportunities** lane | CJM "pain points & opportunities" band (Kalbach/NN/g) — renamed for tone | Canon, relabelled |
| **Current vs Idea** cards | As-is / to-be state maps — standard in journey mapping (Kalbach, ch. 3: visualizing strategic insight) | Canon |
| Lane guiding questions | Workshop-facilitation prompts in journey-mapping practice (NN/g-style) — our wording is our own | Canon practice, our wording |
| **Recurring episodes** lane | **Our adaptation** — makes the campaign/appeal calendar visible inside a relationship map; grounded in fundraising's operating rhythm (annual appeals, event cycles), not in generic mapping theory | Adaptation, labelled |

| Idea priority dots, filter, exports | Tool usability features | Not methodology |

## Honesty notes (read this before presenting)
- **One thing is genuinely novel here, and we say so:** the *combination* of a journey map with a living to-do layer (ideas + priorities + numbers) in a tool anyone can fill. The **parts** are established; the **product** is ours.
- **Benchmark numbers are *suggested*, not authoritative.** The auto-filled norms are soft placeholders to be confirmed — presenting them as statistics would damage credibility, not build it. (This is why the editor labels them "confirm before presenting".)
- A journey map **complements data; it never replaces it.** The map answers "what happens and how it feels"; your CRM answers "how much, how many, how fast". Keep them separate.
- Donor journeys are relationship hypotheses, not facts about individuals. Maps describe the *common* path; individuals deviate.
